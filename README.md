# Customer Shopping Trend Project
The three major business questions:
## 1. What happened?
What products, categories, seasons, colors, and customer groups generate sales?
## 2. Why might it be happening?
How are discounts, subscriptions, demographics, shipping, and ratings related to purchasing behavior?
## 3. What should the business investigate or act on?
Which products need more inventory, marketing, quality improvement, or further investigation?

Breakdown for the analysis business questions:
### 1. Which products sell the most, the least, and generate the most revenue?
Identified top and bottom-selling products and categories.
### 2. When do different products sell?
Analyzed product demand across Winter, Spring, Summer, and Fall.
### 3. Which shipping methods are associated with the most purchases and revenue?
Compared Free Shipping, Express, Standard, Store Pickup, etc.
### 4. Which colors are most popular and profitable?
Identified colors with the highest purchase volume and revenue.
### 5. Who are the main customers?
Compared purchasing behavior and revenue by gender.
### 6. Which age groups generate the most sales and revenue?
Segmented customers into age groups such as 20–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–59, and 60+.
### 7. Which customers are the most valuable?
Examined previous purchase behavior, revenue, and average order value.
### 8. Are discounts associated with higher sales or spending?
Compared discounted vs. non-discounted purchases, revenue, and average order value.
### 9. Are subscribers purchasing more than non-subscribers?
Compared subscription status by purchases, revenue, frequency, and average order value.
### 10. Which products have strong sales and customer ratings?
Identified products combining high sales with relatively strong ratings.
### 11. Which products sell well but have lower customer ratings?
Identified products that may need investigation into quality or customer satisfaction.
### 12. Which products have low sales but good ratings?
Identified products that may have opportunities for better marketing, visibility, or pricing.
### 13. How does customer satisfaction vary by location?
Compared average ratings and purchase volumes across states.
### 14. How does customer satisfaction and demand vary by season?
Compared purchases and ratings across the four seasons.
### 15. Which products have the strongest overall performance?
Combined sales volume, revenue, average order value, and customer ratings to create a broader product-performance view.
## Business Takeaways and Recommendations
Overall, the analysis shows that sales performance is driven by a combination of product, season, customer, shipping, and purchasing behavior rather than by one factor alone.

The most important **findings** are:
### Sales & Products
Clothing is the strongest category, generating about $104K in revenue.

Blouse, Pants, and Jewelry have the highest purchase volumes, with 171 purchases each.

Jeans has the lowest sales volume at 124 purchases.

Blouse, Shirt, and Dress are among the highest-revenue products.

### Seasonal Demand
Product demand changes significantly by season.

Winter: Sunglasses and Pants sell strongly.

Spring: Sweaters are the top-selling product.

Summer: Pants lead in units, while Jewelry generates the most revenue.

Fall: Jackets are the strongest product.

Spring has the highest overall sales volume and highest average rating.

### Customers
Male customers generate more purchases and total revenue, while female customers have a slightly higher average order value.

Customers aged 60+ generate the highest overall revenue and purchase volume.

The 50–59 age group also represents an important high-value customer segment.

### Discounts & Subscriptions
Discounted purchases have a slightly lower average order value than non-discounted purchases.

Therefore, the data does not show an association between discounts and higher order values.

Non-subscribers generate most of the sales and revenue, accounting for roughly 73% of purchases.

Subscriber and non-subscriber average order values are very similar, suggesting the subscription program is not currently associated with substantially higher spending.

### Product Ratings
Some products have high sales but relatively lower ratings, particularly Shirt, Blouse, and Pants. These may deserve investigation into quality or customer satisfaction.

Some products have lower sales but relatively good ratings, such as Gloves and Boots. Their lower sales may be related to visibility, pricing, or marketing rather than poor customer satisfaction.
## Recommendations
1. **Prioritize high-performing products** such as Blouse, Pants, Jewelry, Shirt, and Dress by maintaining appropriate inventory levels.

2. **Use seasonal demand to plan inventory and marketing**, focusing on products that perform strongly in each season.

3. **Investigate high-selling but lower-rated products**, particularly Shirt, Blouse, and Pants, to identify quality, sizing, or customer-experience issues.

4. **Promote well-rated but lower-selling products** such as Gloves, Boots, and Backpack to determine whether better marketing or visibility can increase demand.

5. **Review the effectiveness of discounts** rather than applying them broadly, since discounted purchases have a slightly lower average order value.

6. **Increase subscription adoption** by testing stronger benefits such as loyalty rewards, exclusive offers, or early access.

7. **Evaluate free shipping** based on profitability, not revenue alone, by considering shipping costs and profit margins.

8. Use customer and age-group segmentation to **create more targeted marketing campaigns**.

9. **Use popular colors strategically**, particularly Green, Olive, Yellow, and Teal, while **considering product- and season-specific preferences**.

## Conclusion
The Customer Shopping Trend Analysis project provides a comprehensive view of retail purchasing behavior by combining product, customer, seasonal, shipping, subscription, discount, and rating data.

The analysis highlights strong-performing products, important customer segments, seasonal purchasing patterns, and potential areas for quality and marketing investigation.

Rather than relying on a single sales metric, the project demonstrates the value of combining sales, revenue, customer ratings, average order value, demographics, and purchasing behavior to develop a more complete understanding of retail performance.

The resulting insights can support data-informed decisions around inventory management, marketing, customer segmentation, product quality, promotions, shipping strategy, and subscription programs.
